Atom mapping for mechanistic analysis benefits from retaining alternative correspondences together with their validity constraints. We present a framework based on continuous fragment growth on weighted molecular graphs. A growing fragment carries multiple compatible placements, merges symmetry-equivalent candidates with boundary context preserved, and branches on distinct saturated placements. Successive decisions form a shared search graph whose retained families preserve correlated atom permutations. On all 1,851 Golden benchmark records, a bidirectional single-seed-order search with single-edge sweeps verifies the reference in 1,833 cases (99.03\%); three orders verify 1,836 (99.19\%). A corresponding SLAP sweep recovers 1,795 cases (96.97\%), versus 1,661 (89.74\%) for its uncut control. On 1,807 mutually completed reactions, one seed order uses 7.50-fold less instrumented search CPU than ten. A separate 140-step XYZ/WBO collection demonstrates full mapping availability and additional low-event alternatives, without establishing chemical accuracy because reference mappings are unavailable. These results identify a practical balance between search diversity and compute while preserving an inspectable representation of the alternatives found.
