# N-demethylvancomycin literature comparison

## Verdict

The recursive 11-structure result is **not** a literature-supported synthetic
route.  It is only a complete geometric atom cover.  Several selected
structures are flavonoids or glycosides whose local oxygenated aromatic
geometry resembles regions of vancomycin, but they are not known vancomycin
precursors.

## Published route architecture

The 2020 Boger synthesis constructs vancomycin aglycon in 17 longest-linear
steps from seven amino-acid subunits.  Its defining operations are an
atroposelective Miyaura borylation–Suzuki coupling for the AB biaryl axis,
macrolactamization of the AB ring, and sequential intramolecular SNAr closures
of the CD and DE diaryl ethers.  Two protecting-group-free enzymatic
glycosylations then give vancomycin in 19 steps.

Primary source: M. J. Moore et al., *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 2020, 142,
16039–16050, DOI: 10.1021/jacs.0c07433.

The earlier Boger aglycon synthesis likewise depends on ordered CD, AB, and DE
macrocyclizations and atropisomer control, not independent catalog fragments
that merely cover the final target geometry.

Primary source: D. L. Boger et al., *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 1999, 121,
10004–10011, DOI: 10.1021/ja992577q.

The final glycosylation chemistry has been experimentally demonstrated with
GtfE followed by GtfD.

Primary source: A. Nakayama et al., *Org. Lett.* 2014, 16, 3572–3575,
DOI: 10.1021/ol501568t.

## What remains validated

The immediate conversion of N-demethylvancomycin to vancomycin by transfer of
a methyl group from SAM using MtfA/Vmt is literature-supported.  The deeper
11-structure geometric decomposition is not.

## Required algorithmic consequence

Complete target coverage cannot be promoted to a route without reaction-class
and intermediate-state constraints.  Literature amino-acid subunits,
macrocyclization compatibility, glycosylation order, stereochemistry, and
protecting-group state must be evaluated before ranking the decomposition as
synthetically plausible.
